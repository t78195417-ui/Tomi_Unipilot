01 · Introduction

About Cyber Security

Understand the discipline and what students work towards learning.

Cyber Security focuses on protecting computers, networks, applications, digital services and information from unauthorised access, disruption, damage and misuse. It combines computing knowledge with careful analysis, risk management and responsible professional practice.

Students build an understanding of how systems work, how vulnerabilities arise, how attacks can be detected, and how organisations can reduce risk and respond to security incidents. The field also covers privacy, digital evidence, governance and the ethical responsibilities of security professionals.

This page is a student-friendly programme guide for the Cyber Security programme listed under the Faculty of Computing at Adekunle Ajasin University (AAUA). It is not a substitute for the University's current departmental handbook or official course registration advice.

02 · Official Programme Information

Programme, Philosophy & Objectives

Department details transcribed from the material you supplied. Please confirm that the handbook is the current approved edition.

Programme: Bachelor of Science (B.Sc.) in Cyber Security.

Department: Department of Cyber Security, Faculty of Computing, Adekunle Ajasin University (AAUA). The supplied material states that the department was established in July 2024.

Programme philosophy: To build capacity and develop human capital in Cyber Security to safeguard business transactions, corporate assets, critical infrastructure and cyber operations.

Programme focus: Equip students with the knowledge and skills to reduce and prevent cybersecurity threats, analyse digital evidence professionally, and implement security practices for computers and cyberspace.

Programme objectives

  1. Build foundational knowledge and practical strategies for detecting and preventing cyber-fraud.
  2. Develop the ability to analyse cybersecurity threats, attacks and risks, and create detection code or software agents.
  3. Develop knowledge of cryptography, steganography and digital forensic techniques.
  4. Strengthen critical thinking about cyber-intelligence and security issues, including strategic responses.
  5. Prepare graduates for professional practice, employment and self-employment.

Minimum duration stated in the supplied material: 4 years for UTME entry and 3 years for Direct Entry.

03 · Entry & Graduation

Admission Requirements

Use this as a guide only and confirm requirements against the current AAUA/JAMB admission notice before applying.

UTME entry

  • Five O'Level credit passes in not more than two sittings, including English Language, Mathematics and Physics.
  • Two additional credits from Chemistry, Biology/Agricultural Science, Statistics, Further Mathematics, Computer Studies, ICT or Basic Electronics, as stated in the supplied material.
  • The supplied material lists Mathematics, Physics and a third subject from a specified science/social-science group for UTME, and also mentions Computer Studies. This wording should be checked against the latest official admission requirements.
  • Successful completion of AAUA's science pre-degree programme may also be considered, subject to current rules.

Direct Entry

  • Passes in two A-level subjects, including Mathematics.
  • Upper Credit in an ND programme in Computer Science, Statistics or Electrical/Electronics Engineering.
  • At least a Merit in an NCE programme in Computer Science, Mathematics or Statistics.

Graduation requirements stated in the material

  • Pass all core courses, required University/Faculty courses and electives.
  • Complete at least 149 course units for the four-year UTME route or 117 units for the three-year Direct Entry route.
  • Achieve a minimum CGPA of 1.00.

Course registration

The supplied text mentions a usual semester workload of 15 to 28 units, add/drop rules, and penalties for sitting examinations in unregistered courses. Confirm the exact current procedure and workload limits with the department and University's regulations.

Accuracy note: The supplied admission text contains ambiguous wording about the UTME third subject, and these requirements can change. Check AAUA's current admission brochure/JAMB brochure before relying on this section for an application.

03 · Practical Development

Skills to Build

Develop a strong foundation through steady practice and safe, authorised exercises.

🌐

Networking & Systems

Learn IP addressing, DNS, routing, common protocols, Linux fundamentals, permissions and system administration.

💻

Programming & Automation

Practise Python or another suitable language to automate routine tasks, inspect logs and understand simple scripts.

🛡️

Defensive Security

Explore secure configuration, updates, backups, multi-factor authentication, access control and basic log monitoring.

🧪

Safe Security Labs

Use intentionally vulnerable training machines, local virtual labs and capture-the-flag exercises only within permitted environments.

🔎

Investigation & Reporting

Practise documenting findings, preserving evidence, explaining risk clearly and writing reproducible technical reports.

⚖️

Ethics & Professionalism

Understand consent, privacy, responsible disclosure and the legal boundaries of testing systems you do not own.

04 · Future Opportunities

Possible Career Paths

Career direction often depends on practical experience, communication skills and continued learning.

Security Analyst

Monitor security alerts, investigate suspicious activity and help organisations improve their defences.

Network Security Specialist

Help protect network infrastructure through secure design, configuration and monitoring.

Incident Response Associate

Support the investigation, containment and recovery process when security incidents occur.

Digital Forensics Associate

Assist with lawful evidence collection, preservation, analysis and documentation.

Security Tester

Assess systems for weaknesses with written authorisation, a defined scope and responsible reporting.

Governance, Risk & Compliance

Help teams understand security policies, manage risk and assess whether controls meet requirements.

05 · Student Toolkit

Useful Learning Resources

Start with fundamentals, practise consistently and keep a record of what you learn.

📒

Build Your Study Notes

Keep concise notes on networking, operating systems, programming, security terminology and lab results. Include diagrams and examples.

🧰

Create a Beginner Lab

Use a spare or virtual machine, practise Linux commands and networking basics, and snapshot your environment before experiments.

🗂️

Keep a Project Portfolio

Document defensive projects such as a log-analysis script, a home-network security checklist or a threat-model exercise. Never publish private data or secrets.

🎓

Ask for Official Guidance

Confirm registration, course requirements, SIWES or industrial training details, and departmental announcements with the relevant AAUA office.
